# Fix the citation, relabel "dark", then fill the gaps

The core of the current hardware draft (`hardware/hardware.md`) holds up against official sources. The Arduino Uno R3 is a **5 V board with six analog inputs (A0–A5) and a 10-bit ADC returning 0–1023**, a 10 kΩ/LDR divider does produce ≈4.55 V and ≈0.83 V for the draft's two illustrative resistances, and a 330 Ω resistor gives a red LED about **9 mA, well inside Arduino's 20 mA per-pin rating**. Three things need correcting before review. First, the Oikawa et al. reference names the **wrong journal**. Second, the 100 kΩ "dark" value is really a *dim-room* value, because a GL5528-type LDR reads **≥1 MΩ in true darkness**. Third, the ADC formula uses ×1023 while Arduino's reference describes **5 V/1024 steps**, so the draft should explain the difference instead of presenting one as exact. A bigger problem is coverage. The status table marks series/parallel circuits, safety, power budgeting, drivers and PWM as "Drafted", but the learning content never covers them. Computer components, bits and logic, the fetch–decode–execute cycle, servos and relays are missing too. The research notes give enough sourced facts to write all of these sections. A handful of datasheet figures (ATmega328P absolute maximums, P2N2222A gain and saturation values, 1N4001 ratings, board quiescent current) were read only from secondary sources or not verified at all, and someone should check them once against the manufacturer PDFs.

## Three claims in the current draft need correcting

**The behaviour-based AI citation names the wrong journal.** The draft cites Oikawa, Tsuchiya and Okubo, "Object-Oriented Design of Subsumption Architecture", as appearing in the *Journal of Robotics and Mechatronics*. The J-STAGE record shows it was published in the **Journal of the Robotics Society of Japan, vol. 23, no. 6, pp. 697–705 (15 Sep 2005), DOI 10.7210/jrsj.23.697**, and the first author is Kazumi Oikawa ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jrsj1983/23/6/23_6_697/_article)). The URL in the draft is correct; only the journal name is wrong. The paper is a niche, Japanese-language source, so the canonical reference to add alongside it is R. A. Brooks, "A robust layered control system for a mobile robot", *IEEE Journal of Robotics and Automation* 2(1):14–23, March 1986 ([IEEE Xplore](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1087032); free copy via [UC Davis](https://mae.engr.ucdavis.edu/dsouza/brooks.pdf)). The Brooks DOI (probably 10.1109/JRA.1986.1087032) was inferred, not confirmed.

**The "dark" LDR value describes a dim room, not darkness.** Typical GL5528 datasheets give **10–20 kΩ at 10 lux, a dark resistance of at least 1 MΩ, gamma ≈0.6, and roughly 20 ms rise / 30 ms fall** ([Rapid Electronics GL55 datasheet](https://static.rapidonline.com/pdf/58-0134_v1.pdf); [Handson GL55 guide](https://www.radiolocman.com/datasheet/data.html?di=632245)). Working from the datasheet gamma, 2 kΩ corresponds to roughly 150–300 lux, which is ordinary indoor lighting, so the "bright" value is plausible. The 100 kΩ value should be relabelled "dim", or the draft should add that real darkness pushes the divider to ≈4.95 V and ≈1014 counts. The table below gives the full range for the draft's circuit (10 kΩ to 5 V, LDR to GND). It also makes it clear to students that **the reading rises as the room gets darker**, which beginners often expect the other way round.

| LDR resistance | Light condition | V at A0 | ≈ ADC count |
|---|---|---|---|
| ≥1 MΩ | True dark | 4.95 V | ~1014 |
| 100 kΩ | Dim room (draft calls it "dark") | 4.55 V | ~930 |
| 10–20 kΩ | ~10 lux | 2.5–3.33 V | ~512–683 |
| 2 kΩ | Typical lit room | 0.83 V | ~170 |

**The ADC formula should be explained, not just stated.** Arduino's `analogRead` reference gives the resolution as **"5 volts / 1024 units" (≈4.9 mV per step)** ([Arduino reference source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/reference-en/master/Language/Functions/Analog%20IO/analogRead.adoc)). The ATmega328P datasheet defines ADC = Vin·1024/Vref, where code 1023 means "Vref minus one LSB" ([Microchip online docs](https://onlinedocs.microchip.com/oxy/GUID-6178B82B-1DE0-484B-ACA5-153F78A78E41-en-US-1/GUID-0BF4C5AD-4ECB-479B-A0A9-E33CD8200A60.html)). Arduino's own materials are inconsistent on this point, because its ReadAnalogVoltage example is reported to use ×5.0/1023.0, but that example was not re-fetched. The draft's ×1023 version is acceptable as an approximation, since the two differ by less than 0.1 %. The draft should say so and add that the USB 5 V rail, which is also the default ADC reference ([analogReference source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/reference-en/master/Language/Functions/Analog%20IO/analogReference.adoc)), is a far larger source of error.

Several smaller fixes follow from the notes. The draft's single `darkThreshold = 500` will make the LED flicker near the boundary. Add **hysteresis** (for example, turn on above 450 and off below 400 for this wiring). No official Arduino source was found for hysteresis, so present it as standard practice. Arduino's official **Smoothing** example (a 10-sample running average) ([Smoothing.ino](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/arduino-examples/main/examples/03.Analog/Smoothing/Smoothing.ino)) and **Calibration** example (min/max captured during the first 5 s, then `map()` to `analogWrite(9, …)`) ([Calibration.ino](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/arduino-examples/main/examples/03.Analog/Calibration/Calibration.ino)) fit the lesson's A0/pin-9 circuit directly and can be cited. The image credits are correct: Shoot stufz and Suyash Dwivedi, both **CC BY-SA 4.0, "Own work"** ([Commons: Uno](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Arduino_Uno_R3_development_board_03.jpg); [Commons: LDR](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:25mm_light-dependent_resistor_(LDR)_(2).jpg)). They should link the licence and say whether the images were modified. One more problem comes from reading the draft directly rather than from the notes. Its image links point to `github.com/.../blob/...` pages at the repository root, while the files sit in `hardware/`. Blob URLs serve an HTML page rather than the image, so the images probably will not display.

## Arduino Uno R3 numbers the module can state with confidence

The official store page and docs confirm the board baseline: **ATmega328P at 16 MHz** (from a ceramic resonator), **5 V operation**, 7–12 V recommended input, **14 digital I/O pins of which 6 support PWM, 6 analog inputs**, **20 mA DC per I/O pin**, 50 mA from the 3.3 V pin, and a **resettable polyfuse that cuts USB above 500 mA** ([Arduino Store](https://store.arduino.cc/products/arduino-uno-rev3); [Arduino Docs](https://docs.arduino.cc/hardware/uno-rev3/)). Memory is **32 KB flash (0.5 KB used by the bootloader), 2 KB SRAM and 1 KB EEPROM** ([Arduino Store](https://store.arduino.cc/products/arduino-uno-rev3)). `analogRead` takes **about 100 µs**, which matches 13 ADC clocks at 125 kHz (16 MHz/128) ≈ 104 µs ([Nick Gammon](https://www.gammon.com.au/adc)). For an LDR, the sensor's own 20–30 ms response is the real limit, not the ADC. A floating analog pin "will fluctuate" ([analogRead source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/reference-en/master/Language/Functions/Analog%20IO/analogRead.adoc)).

The per-pin current figure needs careful wording. Resellers and clones such as [DFRobot](https://wiki.dfrobot.com/dfr0216/) list "40 mA per I/O pin" as a board spec, but 40 mA is the ATmega328P's **absolute maximum**, not a working figure. The Arduino Playground's summary of the datasheet gives **40 mA absolute maximum per pin and 200 mA per VCC/GND pin**, with 20 mA as the test condition at which output levels are guaranteed ([Arduino Playground](https://playground.arduino.cc/Main/ArduinoPinCurrentLimitations/)). The module should therefore teach **20 mA as the safe working limit and 40 mA as the damage threshold**, which is exactly the "recommended vs absolute maximum" distinction the draft already introduces.

The LED example is sound. SparkFun's basic red 5 mm LED has **Vf 1.8–2.2 V (typ. 2.0 V), rated at 20 mA** ([SparkFun COM-09590](https://www.sparkfun.com/led-basic-red-5mm.html)). With a 330 Ω resistor that gives 8.5–9.7 mA, and the resistor dissipates about 27 mW, roughly a ninth of a ¼ W rating. That resistor-power figure answers the draft's own checklist item. One portability warning belongs in the "other boards" note. The **Uno R4 Minima also runs at 5 V, but its pins are rated at only 8 mA** ([Arduino Store, R4 Minima](https://store-usa.arduino.cc/products/uno-r4-minima)), so the same 330 Ω LED would exceed that rating and R4 builds should use 470 Ω–1 kΩ. Raspberry Pi GPIO pins are 3.3 V, and the Pi documentation warns "Do not use 5V for 3.3V components" ([Raspberry Pi docs](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/raspberry-pi.html)). An Espressif staff member advises a resistor divider for 5 V signals into an ESP32 ([esp32.com](https://esp32.com/viewtopic.php?p=68290)). On 3.3 V boards the LDR divider must be fed from 3V3.

## Computer foundations: sourced definitions for the missing sections

None of this content is in the draft yet. For the **components** section, the CPU combines a control unit, which decodes instructions and issues control signals, with an ALU, which does arithmetic, logic and comparisons, plus registers such as the PC, MAR, MDR and CIR ([Save My Exams IGCSE](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/computer-science/cie/23/revision-notes/3-hardware/computer-architecture/the-fetch-decode-execute-cycle)). RAM is **volatile** and loses its contents without power, while SSDs and HDDs are non-volatile ([Corsair](https://www.corsair.com/uk/fi/explorer/diy-builder/memory/volatile-vs-non-volatile-memory-whats-the-difference/)). Corsair is a vendor, but its definition is standard, and a textbook citation such as Nisan & Schocken via [nand2tetris.org](https://www.nand2tetris.org/) would be stronger. The usual misconception to tackle is "my phone has 256 GB of memory": that figure is storage, not RAM.

For the **fetch–decode–execute** cycle, the sourced sequence runs as follows. The PC's address is copied to the MAR. The instruction arrives over the data bus into the MDR and is copied to the CIR, and the PC is incremented. The CU then splits the instruction into opcode and operand. Finally, execution happens: the ALU computes, data moves, or a branch overwrites the PC ([Save My Exams IGCSE](https://www.savemyexams.com/igcse/computer-science/cie/23/revision-notes/3-hardware/computer-architecture/the-fetch-decode-execute-cycle); [Computer Science Wiki](https://computersciencewiki.org/index.php/The_machine_instruction_cycle)). Some exam boards such as OCR add a fourth "store" step ([TutorChase](https://www.tutorchase.com/notes/ocr-gcse/computer-science/1-1-2-the-fetch-execute-cycle)), so the module should note that wording varies. A LOAD 10 / ADD 11 / STORE 12 trace on a hypothetical accumulator machine (5 + 3 → 8 written to address 12) is a good worked example, provided it is labelled as constructed rather than a real instruction set and the module notes that real CPUs pipeline these stages.

For **bits and logic**, NIST gives 1 byte = 8 bits and records that the IEC approved binary prefixes in December 1998: **kB = 1000 bytes, KiB = 1024 bytes**, and 1 GiB = 1,073,741,824 bytes ([NIST](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Units/binary.html)). From this, n bits give 2ⁿ patterns, so 8 bits cover 0–255, not "up to 256". A "1 TB" drive shows ≈931 GiB, which is derived arithmetic. AND, OR and NOT truth tables can cite [Save My Exams AQA](https://savemyexams.com/gcse/computer-science/aqa/20/revision-notes/5-boolean-logic/boolean-logic/truth-tables).

For the **microcontroller vs computer** comparison, the ATmega328P is an 8-bit RISC chip with a modified Harvard architecture, rated up to 20 MHz from 1.8–5.5 V ([Wikipedia: ATmega328](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATmega328), citing the datasheet). The Raspberry Pi 5 has a **2.4 GHz quad-core Cortex-A76 with up to 16 GB of LPDDR4X** and runs an operating system ([Raspberry Pi 5](https://www.raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-5/)). That is roughly 8 million times the Uno's SRAM. To counter the "GHz is everything" misconception, the module can use the fact that throughput ≈ IPC × clock rate, with IPC varying by design ([Wikipedia: IPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instructions_per_cycle)). Extra cores also do nothing for single-threaded code, and CPUs throttle when hot ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/clock-speed-definition,37657.html)).

## Circuits, safety and actuators: what to teach and where the draft's numbers fail

**Series and parallel** rules come straight from SparkFun. In series the current is the same through every part and resistances add. In parallel every branch sees the same voltage and 1/R_total = Σ1/Rₙ, and the total is **always less than the smallest resistor** (10 kΩ ∥ 10 kΩ = 5 kΩ) ([SparkFun](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/series-and-parallel-circuits/all)). SparkFun does not state the rule that series voltages add (KVL), so that statement needs another source such as All About Circuits. A worked pair with 1 kΩ and 2 kΩ on 5 V gives 1.67 mA, splitting into 1.67 V + 3.33 V in series, and 667 Ω drawing 7.5 mA in parallel. It ties neatly back to the voltage divider.

**Safety** should state that mains is out of scope. Disconnect power before building or modifying a circuit. Remember that batteries have no current limiting and "can supply enough current to burn up components or melt a breadboard". If something smokes, disconnect it with a non-conducting object and do not touch the hot parts ([UIUC ECE 110](https://courses.engr.illinois.edu/ece110/content/labs/archive/Skill_Safety_ElectronicsLab.pdf)). Treat circuits as live until verified ([BUET SOP](https://eee.buet.ac.bd/facilities/laboratory-safety/sop0526/Circuit_Laboratory.pdf)). Never short, crush or puncture Li-ion/LiPo cells, use only compatible chargers, and retire swollen packs ([Adafruit](https://learn.adafruit.com/li-ion-and-lipoly-batteries/conclusion)).

**Power budgeting** compares the worst-case sum of all loads with the 500 mA USB polyfuse. An LED, a small sensor and the board itself fit comfortably at around 80 mA, but two of those figures are unverified estimates. A servo changes the picture. SparkFun notes that servos idle near 10 mA but **can draw ≥1 A loaded or stalled**, and recommends "at least one Ampere per servo" on a separate supply **with a common ground** ([SparkFun Hobby Servo](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/hobby-servo-tutorial/all)).

**Drivers** are the strongest correction candidate for the actuator content being planned. The notes flag a suggested 1 kΩ base resistor for an NPN motor switch. Through a 1 kΩ resistor, 5 V gives only ~4.3 mA of base current, a forced beta of ~35 at 150 mA. Saturation specs assume I_B = I_C/10, so the recommended value is **270–330 Ω (~13–15 mA)**. The 1 kΩ value remains fine for a ~70 mA relay coil. At 200 mA and above, a logic-level MOSFET such as the IRLB8721 (V_GS(th) ≤ 2.35 V) is the cleaner choice ([Adafruit 355](https://www.adafruit.com/product/355); transistor basics from [SparkFun](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/transistors/all)). The P2N2222 also has a different pinout from the metal-can 2N2222 ([Wikipedia: 2N2222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2N2222)). Every inductive load needs a reverse-biased flyback diode, cathode to +supply, which clamps the −L·dI/dt spike ([Wikipedia: Flyback diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyback_diode); [Adafruit Lesson 13](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-arduino-lesson-13-dc-motors/transistors)).

For H-bridges, the L298 handles up to 46 V and 4 A ([ST](https://www.st.com/en/motor-drivers/l298.html)) but its bipolar outputs drop volts, while the TB6612FNG (4.5–13.5 V, 1.2 A per channel) ([Adafruit](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-tb6612-h-bridge-dc-stepper-motor-driver-breakout/overview)) and DRV8833 (2.7–10.8 V, 1.5 A RMS, built-in protection) ([TI](https://www.ti.com/product/DRV8833)) suit 4×AA robots better.

**PWM** on the Uno uses pins 3, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11 at **490 Hz, or 980 Hz on pins 5 and 6**. `analogWrite` takes 0–255, and pins 5 and 6 may not reach a true zero ([analogWrite source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino/reference-en/master/Language/Functions/Analog%20IO/analogWrite.adoc)). Duty cycle is the percentage of each period the signal is high ([SparkFun PWM](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pulse-width-modulation/all)). **Servos** expect 1–2 ms pulses every 20 ms ([SparkFun](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/hobby-servo-tutorial/all)), but the Arduino Servo library defaults to **544–2400 µs** and disables PWM on its timer's pins when attached ([Servo.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arduino-libraries/Servo/master/src/Servo.h)). On the Uno those are pins 9 and 10, which collides with the draft's LED on pin 9 if the lesson later adds a servo. That pin mapping is widely documented but was not fetched directly.

## Figures still awaiting a primary-source check

Several values were confirmed only through secondary sources and should be checked against the PDFs before the status moves to "Checked". The **ATmega328P absolute maximums** (40 mA per pin, 200 mA VCC/GND, port-group totals) and the flash/EEPROM endurance figures (10,000 and 100,000 cycles) were not read from the [Microchip datasheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/DeviceDoc/Atmel-7810-Automotive-Microcontrollers-ATmega328P_Datasheet.pdf) itself. The ADC's ±2 LSB accuracy figure comes from memory. The **P2N2222A hFE and V_CE(sat) table** ([onsemi](https://www.onsemi.com/download/data-sheet/pdf/p2n2222a-d.pdf)) and the **1N4001's 50 V and ~1 V ratings** ([Diodes Inc](https://www.diodes.com/assets/Datasheets/ds28002.pdf)) could not be parsed. Other figures had no primary source at all: Uno quiescent current (~50 mA), buzzer currents, SRD-05VDC relay coil current (~70 mA, from search summaries), the L298N voltage drop (from vendor blogs), motor stall multiples, the IRLZ44N, MOSFET gate-resistor and pull-down values, and the Uno-specific Servo pin 9/10 statement. Arduino's "Basics of PWM" article and the ReadAnalogVoltage example were also not retrieved. BBC Bitesize and Isaac Computer Science were blocked, so the computer-architecture definitions rest on exam-revision sites, which are reliable for terminology but secondary.

## Conclusion

The draft's weak points are a citation and a coverage gap, not its electronics. Its one worked circuit is correct, and its "recommended vs absolute maximum" warning is exactly the right frame for the 20 mA vs 40 mA confusion that resellers spread. The greatest risk lies in the sections the status table already calls "Drafted" but which do not exist yet. In those sections, plausible-sounding defaults (a 1 kΩ base resistor for a motor, powering a servo from USB, PWM on pin 9 alongside the Servo library) are where beginners will actually break hardware. Writing those sections from the sourced figures above, and treating the dozen unverified datasheet values as a single checklist for one reviewer with the PDFs open, would move the hardware half of the module to "Checked" with little additional research.
